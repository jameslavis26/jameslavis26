# Previous research experience
## Data Scientist in industry (2022 - 2025)
As a data scientist in industry, I have conducted literature reviews into graph based recommender systems as well as evaluation metrics for entity resolution algorithms on dirty databases. To ensure that these methods fit our specific use cases, I would often critically assess each method for its viability on our specific dataset using domain knowledge, and implement the method using raw python, or a python data science library like numpy or polars. I was able to improve the performance of a key recommender system used within my organisation while reducing the computational cost of this recommender system by leveraging structural assumptions about the data and finding an algorithm that best worked on that structure. 

I was also able to innovate an evaluation metric for an entity resolution algorithm. A dirty database is one where each entity in the database can have one or more records from multiple sources, so a one to one match evaluation no longer captures all aspects of the performance of an entity resolution algorithm applied to this database. My idea was to use graph analytics metrics, as well as clustering performance metrics, to give indications of the strengths and weaknesses of different entity resolution algorithms. 

## Master of Data Science - Student project with the Commonwealth Scientific and Industrial Research Organisation (2022 - 2023)
As part of my Master of Data Science capstone, I collaborated with researchers from CSIRO to explore the use of graph convolutional networks (GCNs) for state abstraction in Markov decision processes (MDPs) applied to conservation science. My work aimed to improve the interpretability of MDPs by developing machine learning models that could generate state abstractions without requiring an optimally solved MDP.

Under the supervision of Jonathan Ferrer-Mestres, I began by implementing the state abstraction techniques proposed in Ferrer-Mestres et al., 2020. I then extended this work by formulating the state abstraction problem as a multiclass classification task. Using PyTorch Geometric, I built and trained GCNs and graph attention networks (GATs) to predict state abstractions directly from the structure of an MDP.

A key challenge in this project was ensuring that the learned abstractions preserved the effectiveness of the MDP while remaining interpretable for human decision-makers. Traditional machine learning models optimized for classification accuracy but struggled with "sensitive states"—those whose misclassification significantly impacted decision outcomes. 

## Bachelor of Science (Honours) - Superconducting quantum devices laboratory at the University of Queensland (2020 - 2021)
In Australia, students can complete an honours degree instead of a masters degree for entry into a PhD program. An honours degree has the same research requirement as a masters degree, but with a more condensed course requirement. 

For this project, I worked on detecting and characterizing non-equilibrium quasiparticle bursts in superconducting devices. These bursts occur when high-energy ionizing radiation impacts a chip, generating high-energy phonons that propagate through the device. Cooper pairs in the superconducting materials on the chip are broken, creating quasiparticles which contribute to decoherence in superconducting qubits and pose challenges for high kinetic inductance detectors and quantum error correction.

To investigate this phenomenon, I used high kinetic inductance granular aluminium resonators fabricated onto a silicon chip to detect time-correlated quasiparticle bursts in multiple resonators on the same chip. By analyzing the timing and distribution of these bursts, I found that they follow a Poisson distribution, with an average burst rate of one every 618 seconds. Additionally, I measured quasiparticle decay times in our resonators to be on the order of half a second.

This work was part of a larger effort aimed at mitigating quasiparticle bursts by developing phononic low-pass filters. The ultimate goal of this research was to improve the stability of superconducting devices and enhance the performance of quantum computing and sensing applications.
